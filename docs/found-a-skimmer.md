# What to do if you find a skimmer

A skimmer is evidence in a crime. **Do not touch it, remove it, open it, or tamper
with it in any way.** Handling it can destroy fingerprints and other evidence, may
be illegal, and can put you in danger — these devices are installed by criminals.

If Vord C5 flags a likely skimmer (for example at a gas pump, ATM, or card
reader):

1. **Do not touch or remove anything.** Leave the device, the pump, and the area
   exactly as you found it.
2. **Do not pay or insert your card there.** Use a different pump/terminal, pay
   inside, or use contactless/cash.
3. **Note the location and pump/terminal number** and, if it's safe, the
   information the dashboard shows (device name, MAC, time). Do not linger or
   confront anyone.
4. **Tell the on-site staff** (the gas station, store, or bank) so they can shut
   the terminal down — but the staff are not a substitute for the police.
5. **Call the authorities.** Report it to your local police (non-emergency line),
   or call the emergency number if you feel unsafe or a crime is in progress.
   - In the US you can also report card skimmers to the **FBI** at
     [tips.fbi.gov](https://tips.fbi.gov), the **FTC** at
     [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov), and your card issuer.
6. **If you used a card there recently,** monitor your statements and alert your
   bank or card issuer about possible compromise.

> ⚠️ Vord C5 is an **educational detection aid**, not a law-enforcement tool. A
> match is a *signal to be cautious*, not proof — and confirming or dealing with a
> skimmer is the job of the authorities, not the user. Investigation, removal, and
> evidence handling must be left to the police.
